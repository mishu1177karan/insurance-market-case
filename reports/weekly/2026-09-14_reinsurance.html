Reinsurers Brace for Deeper Rate Cuts at January Renewals

Week of 2026-09-08 – 2026-09-14 · Segment: Reinsurance

What Happened

KBW: Property-Cat Reinsurance Rates to Fall at Least 10% at Jan 1, 2027 Renewals

Reported September 11, 2026

Keefe, Bruyette & Woods said reinsurance executives it met with at the 2026 Rendez-Vous de Septembre in Monte Carlo expect property catastrophe excess-of-loss rates to decline by at least 10% at the January 1, 2027 renewals, extending a softening cycle that has run through the January and June 2026 renewals. KBW noted current pricing carries "significantly less excess margin than it did previously," and one broker's modeling found it would take $200 billion in catastrophe losses, a 200-basis-point rate rise, and heavy adverse reserve development to flip the market from further cuts to price increases.

Industry Context

Historical parallel: The 2017 hurricane season (Harvey, Irma, Maria) hardened the reinsurance market for years afterward — heavy losses pushed rates up sharply and kept them elevated into the early 2020s. Today's cycle is the mirror image: two years of below-average catastrophe losses (Swiss Re put H1 2026 insured cat losses at $42 billion, the lowest first-half total since 2020) have let capital rebuild and rates soften, the same supply-and-demand mechanism running in reverse.
