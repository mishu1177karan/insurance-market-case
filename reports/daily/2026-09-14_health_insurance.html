Insurance Daily Brief

Employer Health Costs Set for Biggest Jump Since 2003

2026-09-14 · Segment: Health Insurance

What Happened

Marsh Survey: 2027 Employer Health Costs Rising 8.2%, Highest in Two Decades

Reported September 2, 2026

Marsh's 2026 National Survey of Employer-Sponsored Health Plans, covering more than 1,800 US employers, found total health benefit cost per employee is projected to rise 8.2% in 2027 even after employers apply planned cost-reduction measures — the steepest increase since 2003. Without any cost-cutting action, employers said their current plans would cost 11% more. The same week, KFF's analysis of 276 insurers found ACA marketplace insurers are proposing a median 15% premium increase for 2027, the second straight year of double-digit hikes.

Industry Context

Historical parallel: The last time premium growth accelerated this sharply off a low base was the run-up to and rollout of the ACA (2010-14), when insurers repriced individual-market risk pools amid new coverage mandates. Today's driver is different — utilization and provider-cost inflation across employer, ACA marketplace, and Medicare books simultaneously — but the pattern of insurers passing rising medical costs through to premiums, then to consumers, is the same mechanism.
